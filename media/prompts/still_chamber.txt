Subject: the chapel as a clay diorama, a SEPARATE free-standing building east of the main building — the main building, its colonnade and its reflecting pool are NOT in this picture. Raised three-quarter angle from the south-west, roof lifted away. Exterior, faithful to the photographs: NO pilotis and NO columned porch; a plain cream-and-white block, at its front-right the tall narrow teal-green glass stair volume and the cream stone tower with a tall cut-out cross, the white side block with staggered window boxes, a glass entrance lobby at the front, a small paved forecourt with a few cars, pines and autumn trees around. In front of the chapel a wide empty paved plaza (the busking space) stretches toward the west edge of the frame.
Inside, the hall is turned exactly like the earlier layout reference: you enter through the front lobby and the hall lies to the LEFT — its long axis runs east–west, the stage at the far WEST end, all seats facing west toward the stage, the entrance doors at the EAST end beside the tower. It is ONE main floor plus a shallow mezzanine gallery (a half-level balcony with a slatted light-wood front and a few rows of red seats) at the EAST end above the entrance doors, looking toward the stage — never above or beside the stage. Interior materials from the photographs: exposed pale-concrete walls with tie holes and a grid of small square windows high on the side walls, a grey stone floor, fourteen rows of red-upholstered wooden seats in four blocks with a wide central aisle, a wide light-wood stage platform with shallow steps, a pipe organ with tall red-and-cream pipes on the stage's left, a tall slender green cross on the west wall behind the stage, a grand piano at the right, a narrow stained-glass strip glowing on a side wall. On the stage a string quartet of faceless clay figures with cello, violins and music stands under warm light; seats mostly filled. Intimate, focused, warm.
