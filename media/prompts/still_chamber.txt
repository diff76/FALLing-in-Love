Subject: the chapel ALONE as a clay diorama from a raised three-quarter angle, its roof lifted away to reveal the real hall inside. Outside: only the chapel's own low white colonnaded porch, the tall narrow teal-green glass stair volume, the cream stone tower with a tall cut-out cross, the white block with staggered window boxes, pines, a small paved forecourt with a few parked cars and a road — NO reflecting pool, NO fountain, NO café terrace in front of it (those belong to the other building). Inside, faithful to the photographs: a big auditorium with exposed pale-concrete walls patterned with tie holes and a grid of small square windows high on the side walls, a warm light-wood balcony at the BACK of the hall — above the entrance end, on the side FACING the stage, never behind the stage — with a slatted timber front and rows of red seats, and on the ground floor fourteen rows of red-upholstered wooden seats in four blocks with a wide central aisle on a grey stone floor. At the far end opposite the balcony, a wide light-wood stage platform with shallow steps; on its left a pipe organ with tall red-and-cream pipes, on the front wall a tall slender green cross, a grand piano at the right, and a narrow stained-glass strip glowing on the side wall. On the stage a string quartet of faceless clay figures with cello, violins and music stands, warm spotlights, the seats half filled with listeners. Intimate, focused, warm.
