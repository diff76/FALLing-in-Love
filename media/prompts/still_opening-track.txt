The building follows the photographs: dark charcoal-grey corrugated-metal cladding, a wide glazed band on the second and third floors, plain window rows on the floors above, and a tall stair tower at the right corner. Every window is frosted glass with a soft even amber glow — NO interior is visible, no tables, no plants, no rooms, nothing behind the glass. Ground floor: one central glass entrance (doors open) under a single flat canopy carrying the Korean sign 창동염광교회 in white letters (the only text allowed); to its right only windows with a low planter of shrubs, no door, no canopy. The LEFT end of the building is the pilotis on slim columns, and under it the cream event tent lounge — light-wood tables and chairs, welcome desk, potted plants, warm string lights — with waiting guests and two staff in orange crew-neck sweatshirts; a small dark security booth at its edge; NO cars parked under or in front of the pilotis. From the roadway a vehicle driveway with a DROPPED kerb (no step) leads straight into the pilotis; elsewhere the pavement is one continuous surface of grey paving blocks. The street has NO bike-lane markings of any kind — plain asphalt with a dashed centre line only. The shuttle: a festive tour bus (glossy cream with a coral, amber and mint side stripe, small pennant flags) stands in a shallow bus bay cut into the building-side kerb directly in front of the pilotis, on the building's side of the centre line, pointing LEFT — the same way the traffic on the building's side of the road flows — with its RIGHT side against the pavement, so its passenger door (on the right side of the bus) faces the pilotis and the boarding guests stand between the bus and the tent; from the camera we see the bus's left flank, which has no door. A street tree in persimmon leaves, late-morning light.
