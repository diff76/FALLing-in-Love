Subject: the departure point at the Changdong church as a clay diorama from a raised three-quarter street angle. The church is a tall SIX-storey block clad in dark charcoal-grey corrugated metal — count the floors: the ground floor, then FIVE upper floors stacked above it, each upper floor marked by its own row of windows so the building reads clearly as six storeys high, roughly as tall as it is wide. The second and third floors together carry one large glazed band of tall windows through which a worship hall is visible — rows of seats and a plain platform, not a café; the fourth, fifth and sixth floors above it have ordinary window rows in the dark cladding, and the tall stair tower at the right corner runs the full height with a vertical strip of windows. The ground floor has ONE entrance, in the centre: glass doors under a single flat canopy; to the RIGHT of the entrance the ground floor is only windows with a low planter bed of shrubs in front — no door, no canopy there. The building's LEFT (west) end is a pilotis: an open ground floor on slim columns, and under it a cream event tent lounge — light-wood tables and chairs, a small welcome desk, potted plants and warm string lights under the concrete ceiling — where faceless clay figures in autumn coats wait with coffee and two staff in orange crew-neck sweatshirts greet them; a small dark security booth stands at the pilotis edge. NO cars are parked under the pilotis or in front of it. The street in front has a shallow bus bay: the kerb line steps inward by about one lane so the festive tour bus (glossy cream with a coral, amber and mint side stripe, small pennant flags, warm lights) pulls in at a slight angle and stops close to the pilotis; the pavement meets the roadway level with no kerb step along that bay, and there are no bike-lane markings. The bus stands with its RIGHT side to the pavement, its passenger door open toward the building, and a short line of figures boards through it, guided by a staff member in orange. A street tree in persimmon leaves, late-morning light.
